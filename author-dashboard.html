Story Edition Guide & Example

A complete story. A shorter reading experience. Discover the format we welcome on Reader Reach and explore a finished storytelling example.

Understanding the format

What is a Story Edition?

A Story Edition is a shorter, continuous retelling of your book's complete story. It preserves the essential characters, events, emotional moments, and actual ending.

It reads as a natural story, not as a teaser, chapter summary, or study guide.

Our purpose

Why Reader Reach uses it

Reader Reach helps readers experience complete stories in shorter editions, discover more books, and appreciate the creativity of different authors.

Prepare and submit your Story Edition rather than your full original manuscript.

READER REACH · SAMPLE
01 · The Promise

The Night the Light Went Out

Every evening, Mara Vale climbed the narrow steps of the old harbor tower and lit the lantern her father had tended before her. The fishing boats knew that amber light as surely as they knew the tide. When her father died, the town council called the lantern a relic and voted to replace it with an electric beacon. Mara agreed to one final week, though she could not bring herself to say goodbye.

On the last evening, the electric beacon failed before its first full night. A storm rose from the east, swallowing the harbor in rain. Somewhere beyond the breakwater, three boats were still at sea.

02 · The Choice

A Signal in the Storm

Mara ran to the tower with her younger brother, Eli. The lantern's fuel tank was nearly empty, and its glass chimney had cracked. Eli offered to fetch more oil from the chandlery, but the floodwater was already climbing the market steps. Mara could not ask him to cross it.

Then she remembered the brass mirror their father kept beneath the stairs. He had used it years ago to throw the lantern's beam farther across the channel. Together, they lifted the heavy mirror into place. Mara wrapped the cracked glass with wire, poured in the last oil, and struck a match. A thin flame caught.

2 / 6
03 · The Crossing

What the Harbor Could See

From the tower window, Mara saw nothing but rain. She turned the mirror slowly, sending a pulse of gold toward the dark water. Eli counted between flashes, following the pattern their father had taught them: three short signals for the safe channel, one long signal for danger.

Far out at sea, Captain Soren recognized the rhythm. His boat was drifting toward the rocks, its compass useless in the storm. He steered toward the flashes and sounded his horn. A second horn answered behind him. Mara's hands shook, but she kept the signal steady.

3 / 6
04 · The Return

Three Boats, One Light

The first boat entered the harbor just before midnight. The second followed with a torn sail. The third had lost its engine and came in slowly, pulled by the others. When the final hull crossed the breakwater, Eli shouted so loudly that the sound carried down the stairs. Mara let the lantern burn until the sky began to pale.

At dawn, the council gathered at the tower. They saw the cracked chimney, the empty tank, and the brass mirror resting against the wall. The mayor admitted that the new beacon had failed because its backup battery had never been installed.

4 / 6
05 · The Ending

A Light Kept for Everyone

The council repaired the electric beacon, but they also restored the old lantern as an independent backup. Mara refused to keep the tower alone. She trained a team of volunteers, including Eli, so the harbor's safety would never again depend on one person.

On the first calm evening after the storm, the boats returned beneath two lights: one bright and modern, the other warm and familiar. Mara stood beside her brother at the tower window. She finally understood what her father had meant when he said a light was not a possession. It was a promise people made to one another. This time, the whole town would keep it.

About this example

A complete reading experience

This original fictional sample demonstrates the intended format: a continuous narrative with a beginning, central conflict, turning point, resolution, and actual ending. A submitted Story Edition should similarly preserve the complete arc of its source book, even when the source is much longer.

Preparation checklist

What your edition should include

  • Your book title, author name, and clear cover.
  • A readable, thoughtfully organized interior.
  • A naturally flowing retelling from beginning to end.
  • The essential characters, conflicts, turning points, and emotions.
  • The actual conclusion of the story.
